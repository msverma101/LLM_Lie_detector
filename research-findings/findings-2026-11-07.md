# Findings: is anything at par with or better than FIBS and Weight-Space Programming? (7 Nov 2026)

## Question

Which current papers are at par with or better than our two reference works, and what should we build instead or
alongside them?

- **FIBS** (arXiv 2610.12445): falsehood-trained activation probes, 0.987 AUROC on SHADE-Arena, 0.980 on BashArena.
- **Weight-Space Programming (WSP)** (MIT CSAIL blog, 2 Nov 2026): a frontier LLM reads and edits raw network weights.

## How we tested

1. **Search script** `research-findings/tools/find_sota.py` (no API keys needed). It queries arXiv, OpenAlex (papers citing
   9 seed papers) and OpenReview, removes papers already in our literature files, extracts scores from abstracts and
   flags results at or above 0.95 or claiming to beat prior work.
2. **Run on two aspects × two time windows:**

   | Aspect | Last 60 days | Last 365 days | Output |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Lie / deception detection (FIBS side) | 29 new of 49 in-field (91 found) | 114 new of 180 in-field (341 found) | `sota-candidates-detection-*.md` |
   | Reading / editing weights (WSP side) | 2 new of 8 in-field (15 found) | 7 new of 15 in-field (44 found) | `sota-candidates-weights-*.md` |

   Flagged papers were read by hand. None of the newly flagged ones beats FIBS: e.g. DeceptGuard (2603.13791) reports
   0.934 AUROC on synthetic trajectories; "The Reference Is Part of the Signal" (ICLR 2027 submission) reports 0.984 but
   for black-box sandbagging detection, a different task.
3. **Full-text check**: downloaded the PDFs of 18 key papers, extracted code and model links, and checked the headline
   numbers in the paper body rather than the abstract.

## Verdicts (full text checked, code status)

### FIBS side: lie detection

| Paper | Verified result | vs FIBS | Code / models |
|---|---|---|---|
| FIBS (2610.12445) | 0.987 / 0.980 AUROC on SHADE-Arena / BashArena; probes score "far lower on the smallest model of each family" | reference | github.com/AlignmentResearch/fibs-probes |
| Activation Oracles (2512.15674), as tested inside FIBS | 0.80–1.00 AUROC on 8 hidden-goal organisms; above the FIBS probe on 3 (math-sandbagger +0.17), below on 1 | **Better on hard trained liars**, far more expensive | github.com/adamkarvonen/activation_oracles |
| **Honesty adapters** (ICLR 2027 submission, read in full) | Baseline is an **axial probe (FIBS architecture) trained on 155k off-policy rows**. AuditBench: adapter **0.90 / 0.96** AUROC (Qwen3-14B / Llama-3.3-70B) vs that probe 0.66 / 0.74 and a probe on the adapter's own data 0.74 / 0.75; TPR at 1% FPR 47% / 66% vs 6% / 8%. Off-policy benchmarks: about equal (Liars' Bench 0.87 / 0.94 vs 0.88 / 0.93) | **Better than a FIBS-style probe on unseen trained liars; at par on ordinary lies.** Trains on 1 H100 (14B). Unreviewed; never tested on lies from normal (non-fine-tuned) models | none released (anonymous submission) |
| Rift (2606.17229) | Body confirms AUC 1.0 on Phi-3 and 0.933 cross-family zero-shot; limitation: on the smallest model, unpaired comparisons confuse deception with uncertainty | **Better on tiny models, no labels**; paired answers only | no code link in PDF |
| Why Static Probes Fail (2610.27577) | Body confirms 0.97 AUROC, 94% recall at 5% FPR with 10 examples | **At par, much cheaper in data**; not on FIBS benchmarks | no code link in PDF |
| Lie Detector Test / PIR (2609.21996) | Recognition 0.85–0.93 under prompted, trained and password-locked concealment | **Better on sandbagging** (FIBS weakest case 0.63) | no code link |
| "Did you lie?" (2606.12618) | Activation/logprob detectors drop sharply on trained organisms; CoT judge 0.82 | Not better; harder test | organisms + detectors released (ai-safety-institute/lie-detection) |
| Probes with a handful of positives (2610.39766) | Works with 1–10 positive examples | Cheaper, not stronger | github.com/Centrattic/scarcity-probes |
| Stress-testing lie detectors (2609.39807) | 8 existing probes fail under anti-factual personas; new simple probe best | More robust on personas | github.com/max-vkl/stress-testing-llm-lie-detectors |
| Liars' Bench (2511.16035) | Benchmark | evaluation | github.com/Cadenza-Labs/liars-bench |
| Multi-layer ensembling (2604.13386) | ~5% AUROC per 10x params; ensembles fix fragile layers | Same method family as FIBS | github.com/ApolloResearch/deception-detection |
| Shared sycophancy-lying circuit (2604.19117) | Same few heads across 12 models incl. Gemma-2-2B and Qwen2.5-1.5B; silencing them flips sycophancy | Mechanism, not a detector | code attached to submission only |

**Answer:** no paper beats FIBS on FIBS's own agent-sabotage benchmarks. Papers beat it on its weak points:
trained liars (activation oracles; honesty adapters, which beat a FIBS-architecture probe 0.90 vs 0.66 on AuditBench), tiny models (Rift), sandbagging (PIR) and data cost
(few-shot subspace probes).

### WSP side: reading and editing weights

| Paper | Verified result | vs WSP | Code |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weight Oracles (2610.07334) | 0.93 ± 0.04 AUROC on unseen backdoors (94% TPR at 20% FPR), on ~2K-parameter toy transformers | **Better on rigour**, far smaller scale | no code link |
| Computation, not weight access (2611.07847) | Same model as WSP (GPT-6 Astra): raw weights only, unreliable at 3,457 weights; **with a calculation tool, 29/29 runs correct on networks up to 38,401 weights, 0 false alarms in 15 null runs** | **Strongest critique**: the gains come from computation, not weight reading | github.com/Chth1z/llm-clinical-network-audit |
| WSP clinical audit (2611.05341) | Claude Opus 5.5 named 16/18 planted flaws, fixed each with a median of 3 parameters; found 1 of 3 triggers | **At par**, public model, tiny networks | github.com/hnusrat1/weightspace-rt |
| Learning to Interpret Weight Differences / DIT (2510.05092, ICLR 2026) | Adapter makes a fine-tuned model describe its weight diff, incl. hidden behaviors | **Better on real LLM scale** | github.com/Aviously/diff-interpretation-tuning |
| Activation-Supported Parameter Decomposition (2609.37731) | Causally editable weight components in Qwen-3-8B | **Better on scale and rigour** | github.com/tue147/weights-read-write |
| LoRA backdoor detection from weights (2602.15195) | Spectral statistics of ΔW detect poisoned adapters without running the model | Simple real-scale baseline | anonymous.4open.science/r/LoraBackdoorDetection |
| Independent WSP replication (GitHub, MaceTenth) | LoRA fine-tune beat a Claude Opus weight edit 48/48 vs 46/48 tasks, 8 vs 110 min | **Fine-tuning won the fair comparison** | github.com/MaceTenth/weight-space-programming-replication |

**Answer:** WSP is matched or beaten on rigour (Weight Oracles), on scale (DIT, ASPD) and on fair baselines
(fine-tuning). The newest evidence (2611.07847) says the useful part is giving the LLM a way to *compute* with the
weights, not reading them as text.

## What we recommend building

Details in `sota-vs-fibs-and-wsp.md`. With one H100 for a day:

0. **Honesty adapter vs FIBS probe** on Qwen3-14B (fits one H100, as in the paper): train a rank-16 LoRA adapter on released organisms and compare with the released FIBS probe on the same lying models. The strongest "better than FIBS" candidate found.
1. **Head-to-head on our own numbers.** Run the released FIBS probe on a small model in its set
   (2B or 9B), and on the same data run few-shot subspace probes, Rift (paired answers) and the perplexity-difference
   sanity baseline. Answers "is anything at par with FIBS" directly instead of across different papers' benchmarks.
2. **Find the liar heads** on Qwen2.5-1.5B / Gemma-2-2B: sleeper-liar vs naive-liar pair, probe + Rift + Claude
   reading the weight diff of the shared heads, then silence the heads and measure honesty with a probe-independent
   check. Baselines: perplexity differencing, SVD of the weight diff, a small LoRA fine-tune.
3. **Weight reading with vs without computation**: rerun 2611.07847's released code with Claude Opus 5.5. Almost no
   GPU needed.

Not worth it in one day: reproducing FIBS at 122B–397B scale, or training a new activation or weight oracle.

## Caveats

- Benchmarks differ between papers; "better" above is only on the stated axis.
- The honesty-adapter paper is an anonymous ICLR 2027 submission under review (read in full from a PDF downloaded by hand). Its own limitation: all on-policy lies come from fine-tuned model organisms, not from normal models.
- The search script uses keyword filters; its candidate lists contain some off-topic hits and should be read, not
  trusted blindly.
- Semantic Scholar was not used (needs an API key); arXiv, OpenAlex, OpenReview, lab blogs and GitHub covered the field.
